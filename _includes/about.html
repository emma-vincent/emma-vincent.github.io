Background & Perspective

Scientific thinking.
Looking beyond the immediate solution.

I recently completed a PhD in computational physics, developing expertise in mathematical modelling, simulation, data analysis and communicating complex findings.

My scientific training has shaped how I approach problems: questioning assumptions, examining evidence, understanding complex systems and recognising uncertainty. I particularly enjoy finding opportunities to improve how things work.

My perspective has also been influenced by Amy Gutmann's work on the ethics of emerging technologies, which I encountered during my undergraduate studies. Her principles of public beneficence, responsible stewardship, intellectual responsibility, democratic deliberation, and justice and fairness left a lasting impression on me.

They helped shape how I think about progress: solving the immediate technical problem is only part of the challenge. Understanding the wider consequences, who benefits, and whether a solution is practical and sustainable matters too.

I believe technical progress, commercial viability and responsible outcomes can reinforce one another, while recognising that meaningful decisions often involve difficult trade-offs.

I'm building this portfolio to explore interesting questions, apply my analytical skills to unfamiliar problems and demonstrate how I approach practical problem-solving beyond academic research.
