01 / The Claim

Facility A is the clear winner. Right?

A regional waste authority is comparing two recycling facilities. Both process 1,000 tonnes of incoming material. Their reported recycling rates are:

FACILITY A
78%

780 of 1,000 tonnes recycled

FACILITY B
51%

510 of 1,000 tonnes recycled

The reported conclusion

Facility A recycles considerably more material. It should therefore be considered the better-performing facility.

That sounds reasonable. But is the information sufficient to support the recommendation?

03 / The Reveal

The numbers tell another story.

Not all incoming waste is equally easy to recycle. Let's separate the two material categories.

Material Facility A Facility B
Easy materials 90% 95%
Difficult materials 30% 40%
Overall 78% 51%
Wait a moment.

Facility B has a higher recycling rate in both material categories, even though its overall recycling rate is lower.

The facilities process different material mixes.

Incoming materials Facility A Facility B
Easy 800 tonnes 200 tonnes
Difficult 200 tonnes 800 tonnes

Facility A processes mostly easy materials, while Facility B processes mostly difficult materials. The overall rates are weighted by these very different proportions.

04 / The Correction

Same performance. Different result.

This is Simpson's Paradox: a trend within individual groups reverses when the groups are combined.

The overall recycling rate is a weighted average. Change the mix of materials and you can change the overall result without changing either category-specific rate.

0% easy 100% easy
Facility A — overall 78%
Facility B — overall 51%

Facility A appears better overall.

What if the facilities processed the same mix?

Apply a common mix of 50% easy materials and 50% difficult materials.

FACILITY A — STANDARDISED
60%

0.5 × 90% + 0.5 × 30%

FACILITY B — STANDARDISED
67.5%

0.5 × 95% + 0.5 × 40%

The correction

Under the same material mix, Facility B has the higher category-adjusted recycling rate. That is more informative when comparing processing effectiveness.

It does not automatically make Facility B the best operational choice. Costs, capacity, contamination and environmental outcomes may also matter.

05 / The Application

Now make the business decision.

You're advising a waste authority awarding a new processing contract. Before recommending a facility, what additional information would you request?

Select the evidence you would investigate.
Think beyond one KPI.

All six measures can contribute useful evidence. Their importance depends on the contract's objectives and operational constraints.

Comparing recycling rates within equivalent material categories helps assess processing effectiveness, but a contract decision also requires cost, capacity and environmental considerations.

The problem wasn't the calculation. It was the conclusion.

Before accepting an aggregate performance metric, ask what is being combined, whether the underlying groups are comparable, and what decision the metric is supposed to inform.
